1. Infographic: neurodiversity myths and facts

MythNeurodivergent people are all intellectually disabled.
FactAutism, ADHD and dyslexia are differences in how brains process information, and they occur across all levels of ability.
MythIt is caused by poor parenting.
FactThese are neurodevelopmental differences with biological roots. Parenting does not cause them, though supportive environments help children thrive.
MythChildren grow out of it.
FactNeurodivergence is lifelong. What changes is the support a person needs and the skills they build.

Neurodiversity is the idea that variation in the human brain is natural (Singer, 1999; Armstrong, 2010). Original artwork, built in HTML and CSS.

2. Blog post: what is rehabilitation psychology?

Helping people live fully after injury, illness or disability

When someone gets a chronic illness, a spinal cord injury or a disability, medical treatment is only part of recovery. Rehabilitation psychology looks at the rest: how a person adjusts, what they want their life to look like, and what stands in the way.

Rehabilitation psychologists work in teams alongside doctors, physiotherapists, occupational therapists and speech therapists. They support coping and motivation, help families understand what is happening, and work to remove barriers in the environment, not only in the person.

A key idea comes from Beatrice Wright (1983): focus on a person’s strengths and abilities, and notice how society’s attitudes and physical barriers can disable people as much as the condition itself. For children, rehabilitation often means building skills for play, learning and communication, with the family involved at every step.

Takeaway: recovery is about participation in life, not only fixing a problem.

3. Social media carousel: supporting a child who learns differently

Support a child who learns differentlyFive ideas for volunteers, siblings and classmates. Swipe along.
Break it into stepsTeach one small step at a time, then add the next. Researchers call this scaffolding.
Show, don’t only tellUse pictures, gestures and examples alongside words.
Give time to respondWait a few extra seconds. Rushing can shut thinking down.
Praise the effort“You kept trying” builds more confidence than “you’re so smart.”

Slide 2 draws on Wood, Bruner and Ross (1976). General awareness only. If you are worried about a child’s development, speak to a paediatrician or child development specialist.

APA ethics and professional behaviour

The APA Ethics Code rests on five principles: beneficence and nonmaleficence, fidelity and responsibility, integrity, justice, and respect for people’s rights and dignity. I am a student, not a licensed psychologist, and I follow their spirit.

  • Stay accurate and cite sources. No exaggerated claims.
  • Know my limits: no diagnosing or treating anyone online.
  • Public content is general information and creates no therapist and client relationship.
  • Avoid harm: no graphic or stigmatising descriptions, and signpost support where needed.
  • Be honest about my role and experience.
Confidentiality and online boundaries
  • No case details: my internship and teaching are described only by what I learned, never about individual children, families or sessions.
  • Examples are fictional or general, so nobody can be recognised.
  • I do not post anyone’s photo, words or private messages without clear permission.
  • I do not give personal advice in comments or messages. I point people to qualified professionals.
  • I share only my email, not my phone number.
Copyright and Creative Commons

In India, the Copyright Act, 1957 protects original written, artistic and audiovisual work automatically. Being easy to find online does not mean it is free to use.

  • Ideas and facts are not protected, but the way they are expressed is. I write in my own words.
  • Creative Commons licences let creators permit reuse: BY requires credit, SA requires the same licence, NC bars commercial use and ND bars changes.
  • I cite every source in APA style and do not copy from websites or other students.
Responsible image sourcing and attribution
  • I use my own work, public domain or openly licensed images, and always check the licence.
  • Credits list title, creator, source and licence. This portfolio uses no third-party images.
  • Identifiable people appear only with their consent. Photographs of children are never shared.
  • Visuals are respectful and avoid stereotypes. Text has strong contrast and images get alt text.
Information Technology Act, 2000
  • Sections 43 and 66: unauthorised access to or damage of computers and data.
  • Sections 66C and 66D: identity theft and cheating by impersonation online.
  • Section 66E: sharing someone’s private images without consent violates privacy.
  • Section 67: publishing obscene material electronically is punishable.
  • Section 79: platforms get conditional protection as intermediaries, while users remain responsible for what they post.

Section 66A was struck down by the Supreme Court of India in 2015. I never impersonate anyone or share private material.

Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
  • Consent must be free, specific, informed and unambiguous, and it can be withdrawn.
  • Collect only what is needed and use it only for the stated purpose.
  • People can access, correct and erase their data.
  • Data of anyone under 18 needs verifiable parental consent.

I apply this by publishing only my own email, using no forms, cookies or visitor tracking on this page, and showing no data about children.

Reflection

Making this portfolio showed me that good psychology communication means turning research into something a busy reader can use in thirty seconds, without losing accuracy. I built skills in visual design, content planning, source checking and writing for a specific audience, and I learned to treat privacy as part of the design, not an afterthought. The hardest parts were explaining ideas like neurodiversity and scaffolding simply but correctly, deciding what experiences to leave out to protect the children I worked with, and keeping the design creative yet readable.
